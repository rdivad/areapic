Basics: pick a tool in the top row, then click on the canvas. Everything snaps to the grid (turn Snap off for free placement). Undo reverses your last action; Esc cancels a shape in progress.
Point: click to add a dot. Click an existing point to hide or show its dot (hidden points still work as corners).
Line: click points one after another to draw connected lines. Tick Dotted lines before drawing to make dashed lines.
Fill: choose a color in Shade, pick the Fill tool and click inside a circle, triangle, rectangle or Shade area. If shapes overlap, the smallest one under your click is filled. Click again with the same color to remove the fill, or with a different color to change it. Shapes you drew by hand with the Line tool cannot be filled; use the Shade tool for those.
Triangle: pick the Triangle tool and click three points. The three sides are added for you.
Rectangle: pick the Rectangle tool and click two opposite corners. The two clicked points are the corners of the rectangle, and the other two corners, the four sides and the right-angle marks are added for you. Its sides always run straight across and up and down. Untick Right-angle marks on rectangles to leave the marks off.
For both, tick Fill circles and shapes first to color the inside with the Shade color, and Dotted lines to make dashed sides. The result is ordinary points and lines, so Move, Delete, lengths, angles and labels all work on it.
Circle: pick the Circle tool, click the center, then click a point the circle should pass through. That second point is the edge, so you can draw a radius with the Line tool from center to edge and label it with Add all lengths. Tick Dotted lines for a dashed circle, or Fill circles to color it with the Shade color. Move: drag the center to move the whole circle, or drag the edge point to resize. Delete: click the circle's outline (or inside it if filled).
Part circle (half, quarter and so on): pick the Part circle tool, click the center, then click a point on the edge where the part should start. Choose the size in Part circle: Half, Quarter, Three-quarters, Third, Sixth or Eighth finish straight away after those two clicks, Type an angle uses the number you type (in degrees), and pick end with a click waits for a third click anywhere in the direction where the part should end. Goes decides which way round from the start point the part runs (clockwise or anticlockwise), so it also decides which half or quarter you get. If you get the wrong one, use the Flip part tool and click it, or press Undo. Close with chooses what is added: nothing (just the curved edge), straight sides from the center to each end (a pie slice, so a half circle gets its diameter and a quarter circle gets its right-angle mark), or one straight line across between the two ends. The straight sides are ordinary lines, so Add all lengths, labels and Delete work on them. Tick Dotted lines for a dashed curve and Fill circles and shapes (or use the Fill tool) to color it. Move: drag the center to move the whole part, the start point to resize it, or the end point to change where it ends. Delete: click the curved edge (or inside it if filled).
Adding color: choose a color in Shade, pick the Shade tool, click the corner points of the area in order, then press Enter (or click the first point again). The area fills with that color. To remove it, use Delete and click inside the colored area.
Label: type text in the text box (e.g. 8.5 in.), pick the Label tool, then click a line, a point, or empty space.
Add all lengths: labels every line with its length measured from the grid. Set 1 grid square = to the number of units each square represents (and the unit name). Then use Move to reposition labels, or Delete to remove the ones you do not want. Pressing it again only adds labels for lines that do not have one yet. Use Lengths as to show fractions (for example 8 1/2 in.) instead of decimals; each length is rounded to the nearest half, quarter, eighth or sixteenth and reduced (so 2/4 shows as 1/2). Angles always use decimals. Change the grid size or scale before pressing, not after.
Add all angles: labels the angle at every corner in degrees, placed inside the angle. At a corner with only two lines it picks the angle facing the middle of your figure. Right angles that already have a square mark are skipped. Move or Delete any you do not want.
Add point labels: gives every visible dot a letter (A, B, C, ...) placed on the outside of the corner. Points with hidden dots are skipped. Letters already used are not repeated, and points that already have a letter are left alone.
End ticks: the small hash marks at the ends of a measurement line, like the ones beside the figure in your example. Tick End ticks on new lines before drawing a line, or pick the End ticks tool and click an existing line to turn its ticks on or off. For a measurement line, draw it beside the figure (use dotted or solid as you like), turn its ticks on, then add its label, for example 8 cm.
Right angle: click a corner point where two lines meet at exactly 90 degrees (this also works at a point sitting in the middle of another line, like where a height meets a base). The square is drawn in the corner you click toward, so click slightly to that side of the point. If the lines are not exactly perpendicular, click the corner, then one point on each side.
Parallel: choose 1, 2 or 3 arrows in Parallel marks, pick the Parallel tool, and click on a line where you want the marks. Use the same number on lines that are parallel to each other. Tick Flip to reverse the arrow direction. Delete removes a mark when you click it.
Move: drag points (lines and shading follow) or labels. Delete: click a label, point, line, right-angle mark, or colored area.
Export: Get SVG code or Make PNG gives you the finished figure. Save JSON / Load JSON lets you keep a figure and edit it later.
